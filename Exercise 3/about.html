Australian & New Zealand Television Market & Energy Consumption Dataset

Storyboard 1 — TV Screen Size Distribution (Question 1)

Issue:

Consumers struggle to choose TV dimensions due to inconsistent retail labeling (cm vs. inches) and an overwhelming range of display sizes.

Demonstrate Issue:

Present raw retail catalog data showing extreme variance in dimensions, resulting in consumer choice paralysis and return rates.

Ideas for overcoming issue:

Convert measurements into standardized inches and plot a distribution histogram to identify dominant market clusters.

Market Insight:

Production is heavily concentrated between 120 cm and 190 cm (50"–75"), while compact sizes (<100 cm) and oversized units (>215 cm) represent small niche volumes.

Data Visualization - Histogram:

The histogram shows TV sizes clustered at sharp peaks: ~140 cm (55"), ~165 cm (65"), and ~190 cm (75"), with 65-inch models hitting the highest market frequency at 871 units.

RECOMMENDATION:

Prioritize prime inventory and showroom shelf space for 55" (~140 cm) and 65" (~165 cm) models to match proven consumer demand, while keeping sub-100 cm and 85"+ units on a low-stock or order-on-demand basis.

Storyboard 2 — Screen Tech vs. Energy Consumption (Question 3)

Issue:

Consumers want larger modern screens but fear escalating electricity costs and misleading eco-label claims across different panel types.

Demonstrate Issue:

Show wide disparities in annual power usage (kWh/year) between basic LED, OLED, and QLED models of identical physical dimensions.

Ideas for overcoming issue:

Segment screens into standard size tiers (Small, Medium, Large) and calculate the mean energy draw per panel technology. Display this data directly via in-store digital display badges (shelf tags) beside each TV so customers can instantly compare yearly electricity costs while browsing.

Comparative Findings:

In the large category, standard LCD (LED) has the highest average draw (~750 kWh/year), followed closely by OLED (~715 kWh/year). However, in the small category, OLED consumes noticeably more power (~230 kWh/year) than both LED (~160 kWh/year) and plain LCD (~140 kWh/year).

Grouped Bar Chart Analysis:

The grouped bar chart compares mean energy consumption across categories. Energy use scales heavily with size: large models draw roughly 650–750 kWh/year, medium models draw around 380–400 kWh/year, and small models draw 140–230 kWh/year.

RECOMMENDATION:

Advise budget- and eco-conscious consumers opting for large screens to check individual model star ratings rather than assuming LED is always significantly greener than OLED, and highlight that compact LED/LCD TVs offer the lowest annual utility cost.
